# MAT_272 — RHT Concrete

`*MAT_RHT` is the Riedel–Hiermaier–Thoma concrete model for impact, penetration and blast: a pressure-dependent failure surface with Lode-angle dependence, a separate elastic limit and residual surface, damage from plastic strain, strain-rate enhancement, and a p–α compaction equation of state for the porous concrete. It works with solid elements and needs no `*EOS` card.

## Keywords written

- `*MAT_RHT_TITLE`: cards 1–5. Only MID, ONEMPA, FC, EPSF and, if given, RO are non-zero.

## How the parameters are obtained

LS-DYNA generates every parameter itself when ONEMPA < 0 (manual, *MAT_RHT remarks): it interpolates between the 35 MPa and 140 MPa concretes of Riedel (2004) for the strength FC, in the unit system named by ONEMPA. A non-zero field overrides the generated value. The generator therefore writes

| Field | Value written | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ONEMPA | −5 (mm, ms, g, MPa) | changed to the code of the unit system you choose (−1 SI, −2 mm-s-t, −3 mm-ms-kg, −4 in-s, −6 cm-µs-g) |
| FC | $f_c$ | in the stress unit of that system |
| EPSF | 2.0 (input) | erosion plastic strain, manual default 2.0 |
| RO | blank, or the measured density | blank = generated |
| all others | 0 | generated by LS-DYNA |

and lists in comments the values LS-DYNA will generate, so they can be compared with the d3hsp file.

## What LS-DYNA generates

Read from d3hsp (LS-DYNA R13.0 SMP double precision) for $f_c$ = 20, 35, 48, 51, 58.4, 140 and 160 MPa:

| Parameter | Generated value |
|---|---|
| A, N (failure surface) | 1.60, 0.61 for every $f_c$ |
| FS\*, FT\* | 0.18, 0.10 for every $f_c$: $f_t = 0.1\,f_c$ |
| Q0, B (Lode angle) | 0.6805, 0.0105 |
| GC\*, GT\*, XI | 0.53, 0.70, 0.5 |
| D1, D2, EPM | 0.04, 1.0, 0.01 |
| AF, NF (residual surface) | 1.60, 0.61 |
| B0, B1, T1, A1, A2, A3 | 1.22, 1.22, 35 270 MPa, 35 270 MPa, 39 580 MPa, 9 040 MPa |
| PCO, NP | 6 000 MPa, 3 |
| SHEAR $G$ | linear in $f_c$: 16 700 MPa at 35 MPa, 22 060 MPa at 140 MPa |
| PEL (crush pressure) | linear in $f_c$: 23.3 MPa at 35 MPa, 93.3 MPa at 140 MPa |
| ALPHA0 | linear in $f_c$: 1.1884 at 35 MPa, 1.0913 at 140 MPa |
| RO | $2.75\times10^{-3} / \alpha_0$ g/mm³: 2.31 at 35 MPa, 2.34 at 48 MPa, 2.52 at 140 MPa (×10⁻³) |
| E0C, E0T | $3\times10^{-5}$ s⁻¹, $3\times10^{-6}$ s⁻¹ |
| BETAC, BETAT | $1/(5 + 3f_c/4)$, $1/(10 + f_c/2)$ with $f_c$ in MPa |
| EC, ET | $10^{27}$: no break in the rate law |

Outside 35–140 MPa the same straight lines are extrapolated (checked at 20 and 160 MPa). The shape of the failure surface (A, N, FS\*, FT\*) does not change with strength, so a high-strength concrete keeps $f_t = 0.1 f_c$ (14 MPa at 140 MPa). Grunwald et al. (2017) found the generated set less brittle than a C70/85 concrete characterised in their tests: the maximum deflection of a slab under close-in detonation was within 16 %, but the damage pattern differed.

## Strain-rate enhancement

The dynamic increase factor is $(\dot\varepsilon/\dot\varepsilon_0)^{\beta}$ from the reference rate on, with no threshold. A run at a strain rate above $3\times10^{-5}$ s⁻¹ therefore never sees the static strength. Single-element checks at $f_c$ = 48 MPa (one 10 mm hexahedron, ELFORM 1, lateral faces free):

| Load | Strain rate (s⁻¹) | Peak (MPa) | $f \times$ DIF (MPa) | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| compression | 10⁻³ | 52.1 | 48 × 1.089 = 52.3 | 0.997 |
| compression | 10⁻¹ | 58.3 | 48 × 1.219 = 58.5 | 0.997 |
| tension | 10⁻³ | 5.69 | 4.8 × 1.186 = 5.69 | 0.999 |
| tension | 10⁻¹ | 6.51 | 4.8 × 1.358 = 6.52 | 0.998 |

Even at 10⁻³ s⁻¹ the compressive strength is 9 % above FC. For a quasi-static check, run slower or compare against $f_c\,$DIF. The model has no fracture-energy regularisation: in tension the stress was still 82 % of the peak at ten times the peak strain, so tensile cracking results depend on the element size.

## Erosion (EPSF)

Elements are deleted when the plastic strain reaches EPSF (default 2.0). In a 3D penetration model, Grunwald et al. (2017) found the depth off with 2.0 and back in the expected range with 3.0–4.0. Their advice is to raise EPSF until the results stop changing, so that erosion removes only concrete that no longer carries load.

## Points to check before use

- Density: the generated RO follows from ALPHA0 and may differ from your concrete (2340 kg/m³ generated at 48 MPa against 2440 kg/m³ measured by Hanchak et al. 1992). Enter the measured value.
- Tensile strength: $0.1 f_c$ at any strength. If $f_t$ was measured, compare; the Hanchak et al. (1992) concretes had about 4 MPa at 48 MPa and 5 MPa at 140 MPa (direct pull).
- Mesh: in a 2D axisymmetric penetration model Grunwald et al. (2017) needed at least three elements over the penetrator radius to see localised damage.
- Rate: see above; quasi-static runs are enhanced.
